Research




Overview

Neurons, immune cells, people and AI agents all form populations of adaptive individuals whose interactions produce collective states: consensus or polarization, coordination or collusion, a division of labour into roles. My research asks how these states emerge from individuals that learn under limited resources, and how to infer what drives them from noisy, large-scale data.

I work on this with three complementary approaches:
  • Inference from data. I have built statistical methods that separate real change from sampling noise in immune repertoires, and that infer beliefs and polarization from text. I am now extending them to track how narratives, and the coalitions that spread them, compete over time.
  • Experiments with agent populations. With my team I have built social-media simulations populated by LLM agents. Because such populations can be run, perturbed and repeated under control, the next step is to use them as controlled experiments: as stand-ins for human collectives once validated against real data, and as systems of interest in their own right.
  • Theory. I develop theory of learning populations using statistical physics, dynamical systems and game theory, most recently on how roles emerge in agent populations. The aim is to predict when collective transitions occur.

Current projects

  • Inferring beliefs from text. Topic correlations in open-ended survey responses on a carbon tax operationalize ideology (Environmental Data Science 2024).
  • Tracking belief change. Regional and temporal patterns of partisan polarization across tens of millions of social-media posts (PLOS ONE 2026), with the Complex Data Lab.
  • Simulating societies. Social-media simulations populated by LLM agents, to study large-scale manipulation (2024; IJCAI 2025), and a call to validate such simulations against real data (ICML 2026).
  • Collective states in AI-agent populations. Roles emerge in agent populations through a phase transition, with a payoff bounded by the information the role signal carries (preprint 2026). Collusion among AI agents is a related collective risk (ICML 2026).
  • Social norms and learning agents. The fitness landscape of social norms (preprint 2026), and coarse-grained descriptions of populations of learning agents (2022; 2024).

Earlier work

  • Neural circuits (PhD, Max Planck Institute, Göttingen). Statistical mechanics of stable chaos in spiking networks (Phys. Rev. E 2019); neuron response theory (PLOS Comput. Biol. 2015); dynamics of recurrent neural networks (NeurIPS 2019).
  • Immune repertoires (postdoc, ENS Paris). Bayesian detection of vaccine-responding clones in repertoire sequencing data (PLOS Comput. Biol. 2020; PNAS 2018), released as the tool NoisET (2022).
  • Decisions and learning (postdoc, Mila/Université de Montréal). Reward rate-optimal deliberation under time pressure (PLOS Comput. Biol. 2022); continual learning (NeurIPS 2022).
