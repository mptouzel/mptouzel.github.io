Maximilian Puelma Touzel

Staff Research Scientist in the Applied Machine Learning Research Team
@ Mila, Québec AI Institute
Member of the Complex Data Lab

I study how collective behaviour emerges in populations of learning agents, and how to infer what drives it from data. Trained as a theoretical biophysicist, I have applied the same toolkit, statistical physics, Bayesian inference and dynamical systems, to neurons, immune repertoires, and now people and AI agents.

My group combines inference from large-scale text and social-media data, simulations of societies populated by LLM agents, and theory of how consensus, polarization, collusion or specialized roles emerge. The aim is to inform how human–AI societies are designed and governed, including the safety of multi-agent AI systems.

Started STAMINA to build community around these directions.

Summary

My research asks
  • how resource limits and incentives shape beliefs and decisions at the individual level,
  • how beliefs, narratives and social norms spread and compete in populations, and
  • how collective behaviour, such as coordination, specialization and collusion, emerges in populations of humans or AI agents, and increasing both.


Four connected lines of research, with an example paper from each:
  1. Inferring beliefs: Ideology from topic mixture statistics: Inference method and example application to carbon tax public opinion
  2. Tracking changes in beliefs: Regional and Temporal Patterns of Partisan Polarization during the COVID-19 Pandemic in the United States and Canada
  3. Simulating societal-scale belief dynamics: SandboxSocial: A Sandbox for Social Media Using Multimodal AI Agents
  4. Theory of collective states in agent populations: Role differentiation as ignition of a collective information engine
I also work to promote standards in the LLM social-simulation field (ICML 2026 position paper, COLM workshops, STAMINA).
